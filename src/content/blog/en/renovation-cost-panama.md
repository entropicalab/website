> the number the municipality publishes is a floor, not a price.

renovation in the republic of panamá has an information problem: almost nobody publishes
what it costs. the municipality of panamá does publish a table, and most of the market
quotes it from memory, but that table does not say what people think it says. those are
the minimum taxable values the dirección de obras y construcciones (doyc) uses to assess
the construction tax, and they exist so that nobody declares a B/. 200,000 job as though
it cost B/. 20,000. they are a tax floor. the market lives well above it.

## what the municipality publishes

mupa keeps a per-m² reference table that sets the minimum taxable base of any renovation,
whatever your contract happens to say.

| project class | mupa reference (B/. / m²) |
| --- | --- |
| single-family home · low tier | 250 |
| single-family home · medium tier | 350 |
| single-family home · high tier | 500 |
| apartment · low tier | 250 |
| apartment · medium tier | 300 |
| apartment · high tier | 375 |
| office buildings | 750 |
| malls and enclosed commercial plazas | 750 |
| open plazas and terraces | 500 |

if the market charges above those figures, the doyc adjusts the valuation upward at
close-out, during the occupancy inspection. the table gives you the minimum tax you will
pay. it does not give you a budget.

## the tax, and the clock

for minor renovations the municipal tax is **1.00% of the combined value of materials and
labour**. for larger work, the progressive schedule of acuerdo n.º 73-17 applies, computed
in brackets across the total valuation:

| project value bracket (B/.) | rate |
| --- | --- |
| up to 500.00 | exempt |
| 500.01 to 500,000.00 | 1.00% |
| excess from 500,000.01 to 1,000,000.00 | 1.26% |
| excess from 1,000,000.01 upward | 0.50% |

projects up to B/. 500.00 are exempt from the tax, and still have to file drawings and
pull a permit before starting. the administrative clock is fixed by law: the doyc has
**10 business days** to compute the valuation, and **5 business days** to issue the permit
once the tax is paid. the permit is good for **5 years**, with a single 5-year extension
if the work is actively under way. once the tax is paid, the contractor is protected from
fines for building without a permit, even when the paperwork runs past those five days.

under the horizontal property regime there is one more step: the co-ownership regulations
and a visto bueno from the doyc's legal technical secretariat, which exists to confirm the
renovation is not about to eat a common element or a piece of the structure.

## renovation costs more than new build, in fees

this is the part that surprises people. the colegio de ingenieros civiles (coici, inside
spia) sets the minimum structural design fee for **reforms or additions to existing
structures at 2.00% of the global cost of the work**. for new construction the same
schedule charges considerably less.

| structure type | coici minimum fee |
| --- | --- |
| reforms or additions to existing structures | 2.000% |
| new residential structures | 1.000% |
| new buildings, 2 to 10 stories | 0.500% |
| new buildings, 11 to 25 stories | 0.625% |
| new buildings, 76 stories or more | 1.000% |

the logic holds up: cutting into concrete that already exists, and for which reliable
drawings often do not, carries more risk and more professional liability than drawing a
structure from nothing. a new building can be calculated. an existing one has to be
discovered.

there are two relief valves. if the renovation lets you reuse previous structural
calculations and drawings without modifying them, the minimum drops to **50%** of the
rate. and before committing to a full design contract, exploratory and feasibility work
can be paid hourly: **B/. 50.00** for a professional with 0 to 5 years of experience,
**B/. 120.00** for one with more than 10. any later change, structural addition or
irregular geometry voids the 50% discount and forces a recalculation.

## labour, and the price of working at night

construction wages come from the collective agreement between capac and suntracs. the
standard crews estimators use for interior renovation price out like this:

| crew | B/. / hour | B/. / day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1:1 skilled mason + labourer | 9.64 | 77.12 |
| 1:1 skilled mason + apprentice | 10.16 | 81.28 |
| 1:2 skilled plumber + 2 labourers | 13.72 | 109.76 |
| general labourer (demolition / cleanup) | 4.08 | 32.64 |

the thing that actually moves a renovation budget is the schedule rather than the base
rate. renovating a hotel that is still open, an occupied office, or a space in casco
antiguo almost always pushes work outside the day shift, for noise, dust and safety. the
agreement prices that: a **25%** premium on daytime overtime, **50%** at night, and
**75%** on the extension of a night shift.

in numbers, that same 1:1 crew at B/. 9.64 an hour becomes **B/. 14.46** on a night shift
and **B/. 16.87** when the work runs on as a night-shift extension. that is a 75% direct
labour premium for the same wall. the agreement also requires paying for rain stoppages,
which in panamá are not an exceptional event.

## so what is the real range?

taking the mupa floor as the base and the audited market valuation as the ceiling, these
are the ranges we estimate with, in B/. per m²:

| building type | basic | standard | premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| single-family home | 250–400 | 350–560 | 500–800 |
| apartment | 250–400 | 300–480 | 375–600 |
| office | 500–750 | 750–1,050 | 900–1,300 |
| commercial / retail | 500–750 | 750–1,050 | 900–1,300 |
| institutional | 450–700 | 700–1,000 | 850–1,250 |
| industrial / logistics | 300–500 | 500–750 | 750–1,050 |
| mixed-use | 400–650 | 700–1,000 | 950–1,400 |

on top of the direct cost, add roughly **12%** of indirect costs on a renovation: the
2.00% coici fee, the municipal visto bueno, and legal. it is a lower multiplier than new
build on design, and a higher one on administrative friction.

you can run these numbers against your own project in the
[cost calculator](/en/tools/cost-calculator), in renovation mode: it gives you the cost of
the work and, more usefully, what the building costs to operate afterwards, which is where
a renovation is actually won or lost.

## what the table does not tell you

a renovation in the tropics is a rare opportunity: the envelope is already built and its
mistakes are already measurable. you know exactly which façade cooks at 2pm, because the
people working behind it have been telling you for years. new construction never has that
information, and almost nobody uses it: most renovations change the floor, the ceiling and
the colour, and leave the reason the air-conditioning runs flat out exactly where it was.

the mupa number covers the permit. the design decides the rest.
